Before work begins

Finish each phase so it stands on its own.

Unlike a recently rewired house, one that keeps much of its original wiring may have few spare panel positions, and receptacles and fixtures that may not suit today's devices. In that older home, phase one may need to include room for later stages.

Write the order of phases down with a rough budget for each, and ask that every stage be completed, labelled and documented before the next begins. A later contractor can then continue from the record rather than guess.

Can electrical work be split into phases over several budgets?

Yes, if each phase is complete and safe on its own. Ask the contractor to describe the whole plan first, then to state what each stage finishes, what it leaves ready and which items wait for later.

Which work should come first when the budget is limited?

Items that affect safety or that later stages depend on usually come before cosmetic ones. Ask the contractor to rank the work and explain why, and note that an assessment may change the order.

What should a quote for one stage of the work spell out?

It should name the scope, the access needed, the exclusions and the written result. Ask every provider to price the same list of tasks so quotes for different stages can be compared fairly.

Which paperwork is worth keeping once a stage is complete?

Keep the written scope, any ESA notification paperwork, product sheets and photographs of labelled equipment from the outside. Each stage record helps the next contractor see what exists without opening anything or relying on memory.

Who confirms the visit and the contract after I send details?

A provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor does. This site of planning information takes in your enquiry for review and handles no visit, price or electrical work. Any contractor you deal with confirms each point separately: first who it is and its licence, then scope with its price and timing. Check the licence through ESA before agreeing to anything.
